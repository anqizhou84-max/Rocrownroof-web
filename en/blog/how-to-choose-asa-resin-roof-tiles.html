How to Choose ASA Resin Roof Tiles for a Tropical Climate

Roofing Guides · 22 November 2026 · By the Rocrown team

In a tropical market, a resin tile roof rarely fails all at once. It fades in patches after a few hot seasons, turns brittle, or arrives on site with tiles in slightly different shades. All three problems are decided before the tile leaves the factory — which means you can check for them before you place an order. Here are the three checks that matter most.

1. Check the surface: is there a real ASA layer?

The top layer of the tile is what faces the sun every day. ASA is used on quality resin tiles because it holds colour under strong UV far better than an unprotected surface. When you compare offers, ask directly whether the tile has an ASA surface layer, and compare samples side by side — a tile without proper UV protection may look identical in the warehouse and still fade first on the roof.

2. Ask what the tile is made from: virgin or recycled material?

This is the question many buyers skip, and it explains most of the price gap in this product. Tiles pressed from recycled scrap are cheaper to make, but the material is inconsistent — strength and colour vary from batch to batch. Tiles made from virgin material cost more and behave predictably. Ask the supplier plainly which one they use. At Rocrown, we offer both: a premium grade in 100% virgin material, and an economical grade for budget projects — and we will always tell you plainly which grade you are quoted, so every order stays consistent.

3. Check colour consistency across the whole order

A single sample tile always looks good. The real test is whether the five-hundredth tile matches the first. Consistent raw material is what makes that possible. Our ASA resin tiles come in six regular colours — jujube red, brick red, blue, grey, blue-grey and green — and we hold the shade steady across an order, so a roof reads as one colour, not a patchwork.

4. Match the tile to the building

Resin tiles earn their keep on houses, villas, resorts and farm buildings in hot, wet regions: they are light for their coverage, easy to cut and fix, and need no painting once installed — a small crew can finish a roof quickly. For re-roofing projects, that low weight also means many existing structures can take the new roof without reinforcement. If your project is an industrial building with insulation or fire requirements, a sandwich panel may be the better tool — ask us and we will say so honestly.

5. Questions to ask any supplier before you order

  • Does the tile have an ASA surface layer?
  • Is it made from virgin material or recycled scrap?
  • Which colours are regular stock, and can you hold the shade across my full quantity?
  • What is the lead time for my quantity? (Ours is typically 5–15 days for regular orders.)
  • Are quality inspection reports available for this product?
  • What do you need from me for an exact quotation — roof area, drawings, destination port?
